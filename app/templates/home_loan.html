How much house can I afford?

Two things decide it: what RBI lets a bank lend against the property, and what your income can service. Then there's the part nobody budgets for — stamp duty and registration are not covered by the loan, and on a ₹1 crore flat that's several lakh in cash on top of the down payment. Parked money isn't repayment — you can take it back — but interest is charged only on what's owed minus what's parked, so it shortens the loan exactly like a prepayment of the same size would. The loan counts as closed when the balance has fallen to the parked amount: net outstanding is nil, and the parked money is simply yours again. A monthly prepayment and a higher EMI are the same rupees by two names — they're kept apart here because people decide them separately.

What a ₹1 crore home actually costs

Take a ₹1 crore flat in {{ default_state }}, the most common shape of this decision. RBI's slab for a loan {{ eg.band }} caps the bank at {{ "%.0f"|format(eg.ltv) }}% of the property value, so it lends ₹{{ "{:,.0f}".format(eg.loan) }} and you put down ₹{{ "{:,.0f}".format(eg.down) }}.

Then stamp duty and registration — ₹{{ "{:,.0f}".format(eg.duty) }} — which the loan does not cover. Your cash requirement is ₹{{ "{:,.0f}".format(eg.down + eg.duty) }}, not the ₹{{ "{:,.0f}".format(eg.down) }} most people budget. That gap is the single most common miss in an Indian home purchase.

And the slab is a ceiling on the bank, not a promise to you. Lenders apply it to the lower of the agreement value and their own valuation, and a valuer coming in 10–15% under the price is routine. If the same flat is valued at ₹{{ "{:,.0f}".format(sanction_eg.valued_at) }}, the bank lends its slab on that — ₹{{ "{:,.0f}".format(sanction_eg.loan) }}, {{ "%.0f"|format(sanction_eg.ltv) }}% of what you're paying — and your cash requirement rises to ₹{{ "{:,.0f}".format(sanction_eg.cash) }}. When you have a sanction letter, enter its figure above and the page works from that rather than the cap.

Rates move with state budgets and we would rather say so than let you budget off a stale number — Karnataka doubled its registration fee from 1% to 2% in August 2025, the first change since 2003, and several states levy a cess and surcharge on top of the stamp duty rather than on the property value. Confirm with your sub-registrar or the state registration department before you commit to anything, and note that several states charge 1–2% less when the property is registered in a woman's name — ₹1–2 lakh on a ₹1 crore property for a change of name on the deed.
